AGI: What It Actually Means

The term used to be a prediction. Now it’s an ad for something you’ll never buy.

By the Meatandpotatoes.ai team · NOV 2026 · 11 MIN READ
AGI IN NINETY SECONDS
What you have probably heard

A machine smarter than people at everything, arriving on a set day to cure cancer or take your job. A fair reading of the headlines. Not what any working definition says.

SECTION 02 →
What it actually means

Nothing agreed. At least five definitions are in use, and they don’t fit together.

When

Anywhere from “already” to 2116, depending on who you ask and how you ask it.

BEFORE YOU STARTA question about your phone

Have you heard of OFDMA? Massive MIMO? Network slicing? No reason you should have. They are some of the radio tricks that got sold to you as 4G and 5G.1, 2

Can you remember when your phone went from 3G to 4G? Did you start streaming video on the bus because of OFDMA? A lot changed, but nobody relates to the improvements in terms of their phone’s ‘G’-level. The phone that changed the most lives, the original iPhone, launched in 2007 on an upgraded 2G network.3 What reached you was what got built on top, and what it cost.

And the label was never solid. It covered whatever the people selling it needed it to cover. In 2010 the UN agency that sets the standards conceded that “4G”, “while undefined”, could cover networks that fell short of its own 4G spec.4 In 2018 AT&T started showing “5GE” on phones that were on 4G. An advertising watchdog recommended they drop the claim. The icon stayed.5

Now, imagine your cell phone carrier selling you 6G, then 7G, then 8G, each on the same phone, each “the start of a new era”. (The first full 6G standard isn’t due until 2029.6) You would stop listening to the number and ask two things. What can I do now that I couldn’t before? And what does it cost?

AI has its own G. It’s called AGI, short for artificial general intelligence: the label for a machine that can do, roughly, whatever a person can. Like 4G, it gets announced. Like 4G, what it covers depends on who is selling.

And the next number is already on the box. Superintelligence is the rung above: a machine that doesn’t match people but far outstrips the best of them. It has no agreed test either. In January 2025, before anyone agreed what AGI was, Sam Altman wrote that OpenAI was “beginning to turn our aim beyond that, to superintelligence”. Meta and Microsoft both set up superintelligence teams that year.7

The comparison has one weak point. Every G was a faster way of moving data. The case for AGI isn’t that it’s the next G, but the final one. In 1965 the mathematician I. J. Good wrote that “the first ultraintelligent machine is the last invention that man need ever make”.8 If he is right, the two questions above aren’t enough. (Section 05 looks at whether progress is arriving as a jump or a climb.)

WHY WE WROTE THISFrom prediction to announcement

For almost thirty years, AGI was a prediction. In 2026 it became an announcement. In March the head of Nvidia, which makes the chips these systems run on, said: “I think it’s now. I think we’ve achieved AGI.”9 In September OpenAI’s president launched a model with “Welcome to the AGI era”.10 Sam Altman says OpenAI will have “an internal system that we call AGI” by the end of the year.11

Most of these declarations arrived as single lines in a podcast or a launch, with no definition attached. The explanations that do exist disagree. Google Cloud’s guide says “currently, true AGI does not exist”. IBM’s says “current LLMs are not considered AGI”.12

That is why we wrote this. The word is being announced more often, with more money behind it, and we had never heard it explained properly. Before long, another CEO or tech savant will make the AGI claim. When that happens, we hope to prepare you with the questions we think are most relevant: whose definition is this, what would prove it wrong, and what does it change for me?

OUR POINT OF VIEW

An AGI announcement tells you which definition the speaker picked, and often what they sell. It doesn’t tell you what changed. We aren’t trying to make a claim on whether AGI is close, desirable, or possible, and where informed people disagree, we’ll say who and what they’re disagreeing about. Figures are current as of 27 October 2026, and every number is sourced at the end.

01Where the word came from

“Artificial general intelligence” first appeared in print in 1997, in a paper by the physicist Mark Gubrud, describing systems that could rival the human brain and be used anywhere a human mind would otherwise be needed.13

It was reinvented around 2001, when Ben Goertzel and Cassio Pennachin were stuck for a title for a book on human-level AI. Shane Legg, later a co-founder of DeepMind, suggested “artificial general intelligence”. The book came out in 2007 and the term spread. As Legg puts it, he didn’t invent it; he reinvented it.14

Back then, “AI” meant systems that did exactly one thing: play chess, filter spam, score loan applications. That is narrow AI, and it is still most of the AI in use. AGI was the name for the alternative: one system that could turn its hand to anything.

02Five definitions of AGI, five different finish lines

There is no agreed definition, and at least five are in circulation. Ask each one what evidence would show AGI had arrived, and the five answers have almost nothing in common.

The definitionUsed byWhat would count as proof
Systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable workOpenAI’s founding charter15Employment and wage data
Matching or beating human scores across a benchmark suiteBenchmark developersA test score
Possessing reasoning, planning, memory and continual learningMost academic researchersNo agreed test exists
“A country of geniuses in a datacentre”Dario Amodei, Anthropic — who avoids the term AGIThe rate at which hard scientific problems get solved
A system generating a set level of cumulative profitThe OpenAI–Microsoft commercial agreement, until October 2025An accounting statement

These aren’t five descriptions of one finish line. They are five different finish lines, with no reason to be crossed together, in order, or at all. A system could beat people at most paid work while remembering nothing between conversations. Another could plan, remember and learn, and still cost more per task than the person it would replace. The word implies a bundle. There is no evidence a bundle exists.

The last row is not a joke. A profit threshold defining AGI sat in the contract between OpenAI and Microsoft, deciding when Microsoft’s rights to the technology would end.16 In October 2025 they swapped it for an independent expert panel, which would have to confirm any AGI declaration.17 In April 2026 they rewrote the deal again, so that the question no longer matters: Microsoft’s licence runs to 2032 regardless, and OpenAI’s payments to Microsoft run to 2030 “independent of OpenAI’s technology progress”. The announcement never mentions AGI. The Verge and others reported that the AGI clause was gone. Neither company has said so.18

None of the five definitions names a technology. AGI is defined by what a machine can do, not how it is built: Google DeepMind’s 2023 framework for tracking it makes “focus on capabilities, not processes” its first principle.19

03How to read an AGI announcement

Line up this year’s announcements and the problem is plain. Each is held up by its own yardstick. None can be checked against the others.

WhoWhenWhat they saidThe yardstick they used
Four researchers, in NatureFeb 2026Today’s AI already has human-level intelligence: “the evidence is clear”20Broad, deep competence across many fields. Perfection and a human-like design not required.
Jensen Huang, NvidiaMar 2026“I think it’s now. I think we’ve achieved AGI.”9Not his own. His interviewer’s test: could an AI start and run a $1 billion tech company? Huang’s caveat: it wouldn’t have to last (“you didn’t say forever”).
OpenAI and MicrosoftApr 2026Rewrote their contract without mentioning AGI18None needed. The money now flows whatever anyone calls AGI: OpenAI’s payments continue “independent of OpenAI’s technology progress”.
Sam Altman, OpenAIAug 2026OpenAI will have “an internal system that we call AGI” by the end of the year11None given. “That we call AGI” means OpenAI decides.
Greg Brockman, OpenAISep 2026“Welcome to the AGI era”10OpenAI’s charter: outperforming people at most economically valuable work. He said there was “no clearly defined AGI moment”.
Demis Hassabis, Google DeepMindOct 2026AGI within two to three years, in the Wall Street Journal‘s summary21His “Einstein test”: could a system trained only on knowledge up to 1901 come up with Einstein’s breakthroughs of 1905?

Read down the last column: a research paper’s own standard, an interviewer’s off-the-cuff test, a contract that quietly stopped needing the word, a company’s name for its own system, a charter, a thought experiment. Nobody is lying. But no two of them are measuring the same thing.

So when the next announcement comes, ask four things:

  • Whose definition? Which of the five in section 02, or a new one made up for the occasion?
  • What would prove it wrong? If nothing could, it is a slogan, not a finding.
  • What does the speaker sell? Chips, models, subscriptions, shares, or books. That doesn’t make them wrong. It tells you how hard to check.
  • What changed for you that day? If the honest answer is nothing, the announcement was about the word, not the world.

04When will AGI arrive?

Ask when AGI arrives and you get answers from “already” to the next century.

  • Already here, say Jensen Huang (Nvidia) and Greg Brockman (OpenAI), each by a different yardstick.9, 10
  • Dario Amodei (Anthropic): what he calls “powerful AI”, possibly as early as 2026–27.22 In September he asked the industry to slow down.23
  • Demis Hassabis (Google DeepMind): two to three years, according to an October Wall Street Journal profile, down from three to five earlier this year.21
  • Andrej Karpathy (formerly of OpenAI and Tesla): about a decade.24
  • Yann LeCun, who shared computing’s top prize, the Turing Award, for the work behind modern AI: disputes the route, not just the date. Language models, he says, “are not a path to human-level intelligence”.25
  • 2,778 AI researchers, surveyed in 2023: a 50% chance by 2047 that machines can outperform humans at every task.26(The same researchers were asked when every job could be fully automated. Their answer: 50% by 2116.27 That is sixty-nine years between the capability and the consequence.)

Anyone quoting “what AI researchers think about AGI” is choosing which number to use.

05Slope or step?

Under every forecast sits one question: does progress come as a slope or a step? A step means there could be a moment worth announcing. A slope means every announcement is someone choosing where to draw a line.

The case for a step. In 2022 Jason Wei and colleagues argued that language models show “emergent abilities”: skills absent in smaller models that appear suddenly once a model is big enough.28

The case for a slope. In 2023 Rylan Schaeffer, Brando Miranda and Sanmi Koyejo showed the jumps were mostly an artifact of scoring. Grade on a sliding scale instead of pass-or-fail and they smooth out. No cliffs, just badly chosen rulers.29

The steadiest measurement points the same way. A research group called METR gives AI systems real software jobs and asks how big a job the AI can finish on its own. “Big” means how long the job takes a skilled person, and a size counts if the AI succeeds at least half the time. That size has doubled roughly every four months since 2023. By spring 2026 the best system tested could handle jobs that take a person 16 hours or more, the limit of what the tests can reliably measure.30, 31 Fast, but a curve, not a cliff, and only for software.

The case for a step still to come. Nearly all this evidence comes from one design: the language models behind today’s chatbots. Yann LeCun and others argue a real jump would come from something else, “world models” that learn how the physical world works from video. LeCun’s company AMI Labs raised $1.03 billion this year to build them.32

For now, the people best placed to announce a moment are describing a slope. Brockman, launching what he called the start of the AGI era, said AGI had arrived “in bits and pieces”.10

06Why the word sticks

If AGI is this slippery, why does the word stick? Three reasons.

Its vagueness is useful. Sam Altman calls AGI “not a super useful term” because everyone defines it differently,33 while running the company built around it. That isn’t hypocrisy. It is room to manoeuvre.

There is always a bigger word. Before anyone agreed on AGI, the industry had moved on to “superintelligence”. Like 6G before anyone has finished with 5G, the label keeps running ahead of the thing.

It can’t be settled. No experiment resolves it and no date falsifies it, because the definition moves. A question that can be answered gets one article. A question that can’t gets a permanent industry.

07What’s at stake

One number explains the attention and the money: people around the world are paid more than $60 trillion a year for their work.34 A technology with a credible claim on a slice of that has no precedent.

The boldest specific promise is Dario Amodei’s. His argument: biology is held back mainly by a shortage of brilliant researchers. AI could supply millions of them, and squeeze fifty to a hundred years of progress into five or ten.22

The strongest objection is one Amodei raises himself: thinking is not the only bottleneck. Experiments and drug trials run at the speed of the physical world, and a faster researcher doesn’t shorten a drug trial. That is why his figure is five to ten years, not one. Critics think even that is too fast.

Promise and objection alike are about systems that don’t exist yet. For what AI is already doing to jobs, read It’s Not How Smart AI Gets. It’s How Cheap.

08And no, it’s not the same as conscious AI

AGI is about what a system can do. Consciousness is about whether anything is felt. None of the five definitions mentions it, and a system could meet all of them and feel nothing. Whether today’s AI feels anything is a separate argument with no agreed test. We cover it in The AI isn’t suffering and in It Could Kill You. It Might Have Feelings. You’re Still Not the Main Character.

So what should you take from all this?

Treat the next AGI announcement the way you’d treat a carrier announcing 8G. Don’t argue about the number. Ask whose definition it is, what would prove it wrong, what the speaker sells, and what you can do today that you couldn’t yesterday.

If AGI does turn out to be the last G, you won’t learn it from an announcement. You’ll see it in what machines do, and what that costs.

AGI has no agreed definition. At least five are in use, and they describe five different finish lines.

3

Forecasts run from “already” to 2116, depending on who you ask and how you ask it.

Ask four things of any announcement: whose definition, what would prove it wrong, what the speaker sells, and what changed for you.

MINI GLOSSARY
AGI

Artificial general intelligence. The label for a machine that can do, roughly, whatever a person can. No agreed definition.

Narrow AI

A system that does exactly one thing: play chess, filter spam, score loan applications. Still most of the AI in use.

Superintelligence

The rung above AGI: a machine that doesn’t match people but far outstrips the best of them. No agreed test either.

Emergent abilities

Skills absent in smaller models that appear suddenly once a model is big enough. Disputed.

World model

A system that learns how the physical world works from video, not text.
